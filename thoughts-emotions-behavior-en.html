How thoughts, feelings and behavior connect

The same situation can mean different things to different people. In CBT, we explore the meaning you give it, how you feel, and what you do next.

By Anastasia Goeva

The meaning we attach to a situation

Cognitive behavioral therapy explores connections between how a person understands a situation, their emotional response, and their actions. In a session, we can look at a specific moment and notice when a familiar interpretation begins to shape a conversation or decision.

I want our work to stay close to your actual experience. We clarify the facts, your thoughts and feelings, then discuss a response that might be worth trying.

An example: waiting for a reply

Imagine you have messaged someone close to you and they have not replied yet. All you know at this point is that they haven’t replied. A thought might follow: “I don’t matter to them anymore.” You may feel anxious or hurt, then find yourself checking your phone, sending several more messages, or abruptly withdrawing.

We can explore each part: what you know, what you are assuming, how you feel, and what happens after you act. This is a fictional example to illustrate the model, not a client story.

What we might explore in a session

I may invite you to return to a moment that upset you and look together at what you noticed, thought, felt, and did. Sometimes it is useful to write these parts down so we can consider them more closely.

We then examine how well the familiar interpretation fits the facts, consider other possible explanations, and choose a small step to try in everyday life. Depending on the situation, that might mean asking directly when the other person is available to talk.

Making room for feelings and needs

Anxiety or hurt is an important part of your experience. We can explore what felt painful and what you hoped for from the other person. Understanding those details helps us clarify what you want to work on.

If this feels familiar, you can bring a recent example to a session. We will explore it at your pace and think about what kind of change matters to you now.
