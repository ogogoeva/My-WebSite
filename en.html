A voice introduction

I’m Anastasia, a clinical and family psychologist. I see adults and couples online around the world. In my individual work, I specialize in anxiety, phobias, and difficulties with eating.

My individual work draws on dialectical behavior therapy (DBT), cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), and personality-oriented reconstructive psychotherapy. With couples, I use emotionally focused therapy (EFT).

What brings you to therapy?

Individual therapy

You can come to me if anxiety, panic attacks, or intrusive thoughts are wearing you down, if your mood shifts sharply, or if things you used to enjoy no longer feel rewarding. I also work with eating difficulties, burnout, self-criticism, guilt, and shame. We can explore why setting boundaries feels difficult, what keeps repeating in close relationships, and what you want from life now.

Couples therapy

Couples therapy may help if you keep having the same arguments, feel unheard, or feel like roommates. Couples also come to me when trust is hard to rebuild after infidelity, when a move, a new baby, or a loss changes everyday life, or when they need to decide what comes next or make arrangements after divorce.

How I work

We’ll talk about what happens in everyday life to understand when things feel hardest, what you want to change, and what step feels manageable now.

CBTCognitive behavioral therapy
We explore how thoughts, emotions, and actions influence one another, then test familiar assumptions and try new responses.
DBTDialectical behavior therapy
We practice skills for managing intense emotions, noticing impulses, and talking about boundaries.
Reconstructive psychotherapyPersonality-oriented reconstructive psychotherapy
We look at how past experiences shape what you expect from yourself and others today, and why certain situations keep recurring.
EFT for couplesEmotionally focused therapy
In emotionally focused therapy, we notice cycles of conflict and withdrawal so you can hear the feelings and needs behind each other’s usual reactions.

Before you book

Booking and payment

To book, message me on Telegram and let me know whether you’d like to come on your own or with your partner. We’ll agree on a time and a payment method, and I’ll reserve the slot once it’s paid in full. Payment is due at least 72 hours before the session.

Rescheduling and cancellation

If you need to reschedule or cancel, please let me know at least 72 hours before the session. I’ll transfer your payment to the new appointment or refund it in full if you cancel.

If you cancel or reschedule less than 72 hours before the appointment, the full session fee applies. If something unexpected happens, we can discuss your situation individually.

These arrangements help us meet regularly, respect each other’s time, and plan ahead. If I cancel, we’ll arrange a new time or a refund.

What if I’m late?

The session ends at the scheduled time. If you haven’t joined or contacted me within the first 15 minutes, the session is treated as completed and the fee is retained.

Deciding whether to continue

The first meeting is a full paid consultation. We’ll get to know each other, talk about what brought you here, and identify some first steps. Afterwards, each of us can decide whether it makes sense to continue.

Frequently asked questions

What happens in the first session?

The first session is a full paid consultation and the start of therapy. I’ll listen, ask questions, and help you clarify what you want to work on so we can agree on goals and a format. Afterwards, each of us can decide whether to continue.

How does couples therapy work?

I help you both understand how familiar arguments or patterns of withdrawal begin and what each of you could do differently. After one or two joint sessions, I usually suggest one or two individual sessions with each partner before we return to meeting together. We agree in advance on how to bring anything important for the couple into a joint session. If either partner is involved with someone outside the couple’s agreements, that involvement needs to end for couples therapy to continue.

How many sessions will I need?

For individual therapy, a starting estimate is two to six months of weekly sessions. For couples EFT, it’s 12–20 sessions, or about three to five months. If a couple is coping with a loss, I suggest planning for at least 30 sessions. The duration depends on what brings you in and the changes you notice along the way.

How often do we meet?

We usually meet once a week. During a particularly difficult period, we can discuss meeting twice a week on different days. Sometimes meeting every other week works well. If you come in occasionally, we’ll focus on what needs support right now.

How does confidentiality work?

I keep both the content of our sessions and the fact that we work together confidential. If we meet by chance, I’ll let you decide whether to say hello. I keep my notes confidential and discuss only what is necessary in supervision, removing identifying details. Please tell me if you’d prefer that I didn’t discuss your case in supervision. Before we start, we’ll go over the limits of confidentiality, including risks to life or a child’s safety and situations where disclosure is required by law.

Can I get in touch if I have a diagnosis or take antidepressants?

Yes. At the start, please tell me if you have a diagnosis, a psychiatrist’s assessment, or take medication so we can discuss a suitable format. I don’t prescribe medication. If a medical consultation would be useful, I’ll suggest seeing a psychiatrist and we can talk through any concerns about that.

Do you offer sessions in English?

Yes, I offer sessions in English. We’ll discuss the format individually before booking.

More questions about sessions
How do I pay from abroad?

You can pay in USDT or by transfer from a foreign card if that option is available. We’ll agree on the payment method on Telegram before you book.

How should I prepare for an online session?

Choose a quiet, private place, check your internet connection, set your device on a stable surface, and turn off notifications. Set aside other tasks, and arrange childcare if needed. In a couples session, both people should have room to speak freely. Bring a notebook if you’d like to take notes. I don’t send my working notes after sessions.

What do you mean by therapy?

Therapy means meeting regularly to return to what brought you in and see what changes between sessions. We can book a regular time or arrange the next appointment at the end of each session. If your schedule varies, try to book about a week ahead. You can pay per session or buy a package.

What might change through therapy?

We’ll decide together what changes matter to you and notice how they show up between sessions. You might begin to notice anxiety earlier, speak about boundaries more calmly, or handle a difficult conversation with your partner differently. I’ll help you see your options, and you’ll decide what to try.

What if my partner or a loved one already sees you?

I don’t provide individual therapy to you and a loved one at the same time. Individual meetings within couples therapy serve the work of the couple. For separate individual therapy, each partner can choose another clinician. If an overlap comes to light during our work, I’ll suggest other practitioners.

Where do online sessions take place?

Sessions take place on Yandex Telemost or Zoom. If WhatsApp or Telegram calls are more convenient, we can discuss that in advance.

What if I want advice?

We can look at your options together and consider where each might lead. If it’s relevant and you’d like me to, I can share psychological insights to help you make your own decision.

How can I get in touch between sessions?

You can message me on Telegram about booking and scheduling. If something I post brings up strong feelings, we can talk about it in a session. You’re also welcome to unfollow me during therapy if that feels more comfortable. I don’t look through clients’ profiles or comment on their posts.

What if I feel embarrassed, scared, or want to stop?

You can tell me a subject feels too difficult for now, ask for a pause, or ask why I’m raising a question. You decide what you’re ready to share.

Can I ask about training and supervision?

You can find my main qualifications in the education section. If you have questions about additional training, supervision, peer consultation, or personal therapy, you can ask at our first meeting.

Can you suggest a different format or therapist?

If I think another kind of support or another practitioner would better fit your needs, we can discuss that and consider next steps.

How do I end therapy?

You can end therapy at any time. If you feel comfortable bringing it up during a session, we can review our work in a final meeting. If I decide to end our work, I’ll let you know and offer referrals if needed.
