Getting ready for your first therapy session

Before a first appointment, you may wonder what to say, how to explain what is wrong, or whether you need to collect your thoughts first. You can begin with what you already know about how things have been for you.

By Anastasia Goeva

Before we meet online

Find a quiet, private place where you can speak freely without being interrupted. Check your internet connection and sound, place your phone or laptop on a stable surface, and turn off notifications. If you have a child at home, arrange for someone to look after them during the session.

If it helps, make a short note of a few things that have been on your mind. They might be events, recurring conversations, thoughts, or changes in how you feel. You can start with what matters now and share more of your history as it becomes relevant.

If you do not know how to explain it yet

You could begin with something as simple as, “Things have felt difficult lately, but I’m not sure why.” Or tell me about a particular moment that led you to book. These are examples of possible starting points; you can use your own words.

I will ask questions and help clarify what is troubling you and what you would like to change. You can also tell me when a subject feels too difficult to discuss for now.

What we will talk about

We will get to know one another and explore what brings you in and what you hope for from therapy. If you have seen a therapist before, you can share what helped and what did not work for you. Please mention any diagnosis, prescribed medication, or psychiatrist’s assessment at the start.

My first appointment is a full-length, paid session. Towards the end, we can discuss how working together might look. Each of us can decide whether to continue.

Questions you can ask

You are welcome to ask how I work, how often we might meet, how confidentiality works, and what happens if you need to reschedule. We can also discuss how we will notice change as the work develops.

You can find the main practical arrangements under “Before you book” and session lengths and fees under “Session formats and fees” on my website. We agree on a time and payment method before the appointment.

After the session

You might give yourself a little time to reflect on whether you could talk about what mattered, whether the process made sense, and what questions remain. If you have doubts, we can discuss them. The first meeting gives us a chance to get to know one another and decide whether working together feels right.
